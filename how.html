How sure are we?

The map is a set of claims: this moves that, by about this much. Each claim carries one of four grades. The grade says where the number came from.

Measured 358 of 1,038

The link's size was estimated from daily prices, with the industry's other parents held fixed. It is an association in the data, not proof of cause, and the instrument is a tradeable stand-in for the driver.

Cited 3 of 1,038

A published study supports the link. We quote the source and say what it covered.

Judgement 614 of 1,038

Someone reasoned that the link exists and typed a number. No study sets it and we have not measured it. This is most of the map today.

Contradicted 63 of 1,038

We measured the link and the data points the other way from the number on the map. The link stays visible with both numbers until it is settled.

What the numbers are

A weight is a signed number. The sign says whether the target tends to move with the source or against it. The size is relative strength inside this map, not a unit of return. 4 of 1,038 weights fall outside −1 to +1.

A shock number is what the map gives when one driver is moved and the move is passed along every link, in units of the map's own scale (σ). It ranks who is exposed and in which direction. It is not a forecast of prices, and tests of the map against prices have so far not shown that it predicts them.

What is left out

This site shows the map and the research written from it. It leaves out anything about any portfolio, and reports that are business documents or that list specific trades. Where a report is missing, the page says “not published”.

Who wrote this

The research is produced by an AI research system under one person’s direction, and reviewed unevenly. Treat every page as a working note. Research notes produced with AI assistance. Not investment advice. May be wrong or out of date.
