How to read it

A link says the target tends to move when the source moves. Its weight is the move of the target, in standard deviations (σ), for a move of +1σ in the source. Green raises, red lowers. The line style is the evidence grade: solid measured, dashed cited, dotted judgement, broken where our own measurement contradicts it. What the grades mean.

A node is a macro driver (diamond) or an industry (circle, sized by the market value of the companies tracked in it). Groups are drawn as patches, with the drivers in the middle.

Companies and funds sit around the industry that holds most of them. A share is the company’s part of the industry’s tracked market value. A company’s β is measured from about three years of weekly returns against the price series of every industry it belongs to. No company-to-company link is drawn; there is no cited supply-chain dataset behind one yet.

A shock moves one driver and passes the move along every link. An industry’s number is its propagated move in σ. A company’s or a fund’s number is the sum of its β times each of its industries’ moves. These rank exposure and direction. They are not forecasts of returns.

CORUM is a research map of how macro forces reach industries. This site is a snapshot taken on 19 Nov 2026; it does not update by itself.
