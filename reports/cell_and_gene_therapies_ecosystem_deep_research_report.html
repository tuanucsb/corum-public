Executive Summary & Core Macro Theses

The biopharmaceutical sector is undergoing a generational paradigm shift from chronic disease management (small molecules and monoclonal antibodies administered weekly or monthly for life) to durable, potentially curative cellular and genomic interventions administered as a single, one-time dose. However, translating breakthrough molecular biology into sustained enterprise value and equity returns has exposed a complex web of structural, biological, operational, and macroeconomic frictions.

Between the speculative zero-interest-rate bubble of 2020–2021 and the higher-for-longer rate regime of 2024–2026, the Cell and Gene Therapy (CGT) sector underwent a violent capital-cycle shakeout. Today, the ecosystem is governed by five interlocking macro and structural theses:

1. The Cost of Capital Filter & Duration Sensitivity

Cell and gene therapy developers represent the longest-duration equity assets on public exchanges. Pre-commercial biotechs require 7 to 12 years and $1.0B to $1.5B in cumulative CapEx/OpEx to bring an ex-vivo cell therapy or in-vivo gene therapy through preclinical optimization, IND-enabling toxicology, Chemistry Manufacturing and Controls (CMC) scale-up, and Phase 1–3 pivotal trials.

  • In a 4.0%–5.0% risk-free rate regime (FED_RATE / LONG_RATE), distant, uncommitted terminal cash flows are heavily penalized by discount factors.
  • Companies without 18–24 months of cash runway or non-dilutive commercial partnerships have faced catastrophic dilutive secondary offerings, down-rounds, reverse splits, or liquidation (e.g., bluebird bio's ongoing debt distress and restructuring). Conversely, platforms with multi-billion cash war chests (Vertex, CRISPR Therapeutics) or validated commercial royalties trade at substantial scarcity premiums.

2. The "Curative Commercial Paradox" & Pricing Friction

A therapy that permanently cures a disease destroys its own addressable prevalence pool. In monogenic diseases with fixed incident populations (e.g., Severe Sickle Cell Disease, Beta-Thalassemia, Spinal Muscular Atrophy, Metachromatic Leukodystrophy), the addressable market shrinks after treating prevalent patients, leaving only annual births.

  • To amortize heavy upfront R&D and bespoke manufacturing costs, manufacturers price these therapies between $2.2M and $4.25M per dose (e.g., Vertex/CRISPR's Casgevy at $2.2M; bluebird bio's Lyfgenia at $3.1M; Sarepta's Elevidys at $3.2M; CSL/uniQure's Hemgenix at $3.5M; Orchard/Kyowa Kirin's Lenmeldy at $4.25M).
  • These multi-million-dollar price tags collide violently with fragmented reimbursement architectures: state Medicaid budgetary caps, hospital inpatient prospective payment system (IPPS MS-DRG) reimbursement caps, and commercial payer prior authorization latency (60 to 90 days), resulting in agonizingly slow initial commercial revenue ramps even after landmark FDA approvals.

3. The Great Patent Cliff ($200B+ LOE) as Inelastic Big Pharma M&A Demand

Between 2025 and 2030, Big Pharma faces the largest patent expiration cliff in pharmaceutical history, with more than $200 billion in annual revenue losing exclusivity (LOE) across mega-blockbusters including Keytruda (Merck), Eliquis (BMS/Pfizer), Opdivo (BMS), Prolia/Xgeva (Amgen), and Stelara (J&J).

  • Large-cap biopharma balance sheets hold hundreds of billions in dry powder that cannot be deployed organically fast enough.
  • Large pharma has decisively moved to acquire clinically de-risked Phase 2/3 CGT platforms with clean safety profiles and established CMC (e.g., Novartis acquiring Mariana Oncology and AveXis; Pfizer acquiring Bamboo; Gilead acquiring Kite; Sanofi acquiring Inhibrx assets; Roche partnering/licensing with Sarepta and Dyno Therapeutics).

4. Modality Evolution: The In-Vivo & Autoimmune Frontier

The sector is rapidly transitioning beyond first-generation autologous oncology CAR-Ts and localized AAV gene additions:

  • The Autoimmune Revolution: CD19- and BCMA-directed CAR-T cell therapies are demonstrating stunning deep B-cell depletion and immune "reset" in severe autoimmune diseases (Systemic Lupus Erythematosus [SLE], Systemic Sclerosis [SSc], Idiopathic Inflammatory Myositis [IIM], and Myasthenia Gravis). This expands the cell therapy total addressable market (TAM) from small, relapsed/refractory hematologic oncology niches into multi-billion-dollar chronic autoimmune populations (led by Cabaletta Bio, Kyverna, BMS, and Novartis).
  • The Shift from Ex-Vivo to In-Vivo: Bespoke ex-vivo cell engineering (requiring patient leukapheresis, centralized cell processing, and heavy lymphodepletion chemotherapy) is an operational bottleneck. The frontier is systemic in-vivo delivery using targeted Lipid Nanoparticles (LNPs) or engineered viral capsids to deliver CRISPR base or prime editors directly to the liver, bone marrow, or immune cells via standard intravenous infusion (spearheaded by Intellia, Beam, and Verve).

5. Geopolitics & The BIOSECURE Act: The Western CDMO Realignment

The passage of the US BIOSECURE Act has codified strict bans on federal contracting and procurement from Chinese biotechnology companies of concern (specifically naming WuXi AppTec, WuXi Biologics, and Complete Genomics).

  • Historically, over 70% of early-stage US cell and gene biotechs relied on WuXi for plasmid DNA (pDNA) synthesis, viral vector development, and cell line engineering.
  • This legislative mandate has triggered an urgent, multi-year supply chain relocation toward tier-one Western Contract Development and Manufacturing Organizations (CDMOs) and tools suppliers—primarily Lonza, Catalent (under Novo Holdings ownership), Thermo Fisher Scientific (Patheon/Brammer), Danaher (Aldevron/Cytiva), Charles River Laboratories, and Oxford Biomedica.

The 7 Structural Layers Defined:

  1. Layer 1: Upstream Genetic Inputs, Reagents & Electroporation Tools

    • The Physical Inputs: High-grade Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) plasmid DNA (the starting template for AAV and lentiviral vectors), synthetic single-guide RNAs (sgRNAs), clean-capped mRNA transcripts, and recombinant human cytokines (IL-2, IL-7, IL-15 for T-cell expansion).
    • Electroporation Standard: Ex-vivo cell engineering relies on transient electrical permeabilization of cell membranes. MaxCyte (MXCT) commands an effective monopoly with its Flow Electroporation technology, licensed to nearly all major commercial CAR-T and CRISPR clinical programs.

  2. Layer 2: Delivery Vectors & Packaging Architectures (The Ultimate Chokepoint)

    • Recombinant Adeno-Associated Virus (rAAV): The premier in-vivo gene delivery vehicle (serotypes AAV2, AAV5, AAV8, AAV9, AAVrh74). Limitations: ~4.7 kilobase (kb) packaging capacity, inability to re-dose due to neutralizing antibodies (30%–60% population seroprevalence), and dose-dependent systemic toxicity.
    • Lentiviral Vectors (LVV): Integrating retroviral vectors used predominantly for ex-vivo hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) and CAR-T modification (~8–9 kb capacity).
    • Lipid Nanoparticles (LNPs): Four-component synthetic lipid formulations (ionizable lipid, PEGylated lipid, helper phospholipid, cholesterol). Unconstrained payload capacity, redosable, transient expression (ideal for CRISPR mRNA + sgRNA), but naturally clear to the liver (hepatic tropism).

  3. Layer 3: Gene Editing Engines & Cell Engineering Modalities

    • 1st Generation (Nucleases): Zinc Finger Nucleases (Sangamo) and TALENs (Cellectis) requiring custom protein engineering for each DNA target.
    • 2nd Generation (CRISPR-Cas9): RNA-guided endonuclease causing double-strand breaks (DSBs). High editing efficiency, but carries risks of large chromosomal deletions, translocations, and p53 activation.
    • 3rd Generation (Base Editing): Catalytically impaired Cas nickase fused to a deaminase enzyme (cytidine or adenosine deaminase). Converts single base pairs ($C \rightarrow T$ or $A \rightarrow G$) without double-strand breaks, drastically reducing genotoxicity.
    • 4th Generation (Prime Editing): Engineered reverse transcriptase fused to Cas9 nickase guided by pegRNA; capable of all 12 base-to-base conversions, insertions, and deletions without double-strand breaks.
    • Cellular Modalities:
      • Autologous CAR-T: Patient-derived T-cells engineered with chimeric antigen receptors (CD19, BCMA). Efficacy is proven, but requires a complex, multi-week "vein-to-vein" process.
      • Allogeneic "Off-the-Shelf" CAR-T: Healthy donor T-cells or induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) gene-edited to delete endogenous TCR (preventing Graft-versus-Host Disease / GvHD) and HLA Class I/II (preventing host immune rejection).
      • TCR-T & TILs: Engineered T-cell receptors targeting intracellular neoantigens presented on HLA (Adaptimmune's Tecelra); Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocytes extracted from patient resected tumors, expanded ex-vivo (Iovance's Amtagvi).

  4. Layer 4: CDMO Manufacturing & Bioprocessing Infrastructure

    • Specialized biomanufacturing requiring Class A/B cleanrooms, suspension bioreactors (up to 2,000L for AAV), analytical ultracentrifugation for full-vs-empty capsid separation, and sterile fill-finish.
    • Dominated by Lonza, Catalent, Thermo Fisher (Patheon), and Danaher (Aldevron). Highly consolidated and capital-intensive.

  5. Layer 5: Therapeutic Sponsors (Big Pharma Anchors & Listed Biotechs)

    • The corporate entities bearing clinical trial execution, regulatory approval, intellectual property defense, and commercialization.
    • Bifurcated between profitable biopharma cash compounders (Vertex, Gilead, Sarepta, Alnylam) and burn-rate-dependent pure-plays (CRISPR Therapeutics, Intellia, Beam, Cabaletta, Legend).

  6. Layer 6: Clinical Site Delivery & Cryogenic Cold Chain

    • Authorized Treatment Centers (ATCs): Complex cell and gene therapies cannot be dispensed at local pharmacies; they require FACT-accredited (Foundation for the Accreditation of Cellular Therapy) Academic Medical Centers equipped with apheresis units, cryogenic storage, cell thawing facilities, and Intensive Care Units trained to manage Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS) and Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS).
    • Cryogenic Logistics: Liquid nitrogen dry shippers maintaining $-150^\circ\text{C}$ to $-196^\circ\text{C}$ under real-time GPS and temperature telemetry. Cryoport (CYRX) is the near-monopoly standard for commercial CGT logistics.

  7. Layer 7: Payer, CMS & Reimbursement Gateways

    • The ultimate economic clearinghouse. Operates via the CMS CGT Access Model (Medicaid outcomes-based multi-state purchasing), commercial value-based risk agreements, and inpatient Medicare Severity Diagnosis Related Group (MS-DRG) add-ons.

  • Enterprise Profile: The dominant commercial and balance sheet anchor in cellular and genomic medicine. Vertex generates over $10B annually in near-monopoly cash flow from its cystic fibrosis (CF) franchise (Trikafta/Kalydeco), which it uses to fund high-risk, paradigm-shifting genetic modalities.

2. Gilead Sciences, Inc. (NASDAQ: GILD) / Kite Pharma

  • Enterprise Profile: The undisputed global commercial leader in oncology cell therapy. Acquired Kite Pharma in 2017 for $11.9B, turning Kite into an internal cell therapy powerhouse.
  • Core CGT Assets:

    • Tecartus (brexucabtagene autoleucel): CD19-directed CAR-T for mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) and adult acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL).
    • Together, Kite's CAR-T franchise generates >$1.8B in annual recurring revenue, operating dedicated high-throughput manufacturing plants in El Segundo (CA), Oceanside (CA), and Amsterdam (Netherlands) with an industry-leading median turnaround time of 16 days and a 96% manufacturing success rate.
  • Strategic Moat: Kite's operational turnaround speed and established network of >400 authorized treatment centers globally create a formidable competitive barrier against emerging cell therapy rivals.

3. Sarepta Therapeutics, Inc. (NASDAQ: SRPT)

  • Enterprise Profile: The premier commercial-stage rare disease gene therapy biotech. Transitioned from phosphorodiamidate morpholino oligomer (PMO) exon-skipping drugs into the leading commercial AAV gene therapy franchise.
  • Core CGT Assets:

In mid-2024, the FDA granted landmark expanded approval for ambulatory and non-ambulatory DMD patients aged 4 and older, unlocking an addressable US/EU market exceeding $3B–$4B in peak annual sales.

    • Partnered ex-US with Roche (RHHBY), which paid Sarepta $1.15B upfront and funds 50% of global development costs in exchange for ex-US commercial rights.
  • Vulnerabilities: Severe concentration risk in Elevidys; high-dose AAV safety monitoring (acute liver injury, immune-mediated myositis, myocarditis); reliance on Catalent's viral vector manufacturing capacity.

4. Alnylam Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (NASDAQ: ALNY)

  • Enterprise Profile: The global monopoly in RNA interference (RNAi). While technically an oligonucleotide modality rather than gene editing, Alnylam's delivery platform represents the primary alternative to permanent genomic alteration for genetic diseases.
  • Core CGT Assets:

Following the landmark HELIOS-B Phase 3 trial victory in ATTR amyloidosis with cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM), Amvuttra demonstrated statistically significant reductions in all-cause mortality and cardiovascular hospitalizations, positioning the drug as a $5B+ mega-blockbuster.
    • Commercial portfolio includes Onpattro, Givlaari, Oxlumo, and partnered assets (Leqvio via Novartis for hypercholesterolemia).
  • Strategic Moat: The GalNAc conjugate delivery platform allows subcutaneous injection once every three to six months with direct hepatocyte uptake via the asialoglycoprotein receptor (ASGPR), bypassing the systemic toxicity and complex hospital infrastructure required by AAV or CAR-T.

2. Legend Biotech Corporation (NASDAQ: LEGN)

3. Intellia Therapeutics, Inc. (NASDAQ: NTLA)

  • Technology & Platform: Founded by Nobel laureate Jennifer Doudna. The undisputed global pioneer in systemic in-vivo CRISPR editing. While competitors initially focused on ex-vivo cell manipulation, Intellia solved systemic IV administration using targeted LNPs.
  • Pivotal Pipeline:
    • NTLA-2001 (In-Vivo TTR Knockout): Partnered with Regeneron (REGN) (75% Intellia / 25% Regeneron). Administered as a single 2-hour IV infusion. Achieves >90% sustained reduction in serum TTR protein via targeted CRISPR knockout of the TTR gene in hepatocytes. In active Phase 3 pivotal trials (MAGNITUDE) for ATTR cardiomyopathy.
    • NTLA-2002 (In-Vivo KLKB1 Knockout): Targeting Hereditary Angioedema (HAE). Phase 2 data demonstrated a 98% reduction in monthly swelling attack rate, with many patients completely attack-free. Offers a functional permanent cure that could eliminate lifelong prophylactic injections (e.g., Takeda's Takhzyro). In Phase 3 (HAELO).
  • Strategic Moat: Exclusive ownership of proprietary modular LNP platforms optimized for liver delivery; multi-program commercial alliance with Regeneron.

4. Beam Therapeutics Inc. (NASDAQ: BEAM)

  • Technology & Platform: Founded by base editing pioneers David Liu, Keith Joung, and Feng Zhang. Beam is the leader in Precision Base Editing (cytosine base editors / CBE and adenine base editors / ABE).
  • The Precision Advantage: Traditional CRISPR-Cas9 cuts both strands of DNA, creating unpredictable insertions/deletions (indels) and chromosomal rearrangements. Base editing chemically converts target nucleotides ($C \cdot G \rightarrow T \cdot A$ or $A \cdot T \rightarrow G \cdot C$) with single-base resolution without cutting the DNA backbone.
  • Core Programs:
    • BEAM-101 (Sickle Cell Disease): Base editor engineered to recreate hereditary persistence of fetal hemoglobin ($HPFH$) mutations in the HBG1/HBG2 promoters. Eliminates DSB translocations while producing high, uniform $HbF$ induction.
    • BEAM-302 (In-Vivo Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency / AATD): Systemic LNP delivering base editor directly to hepatocytes to correct the pathogenic PiZ mutation ($E342K$), simultaneously restoring functional circulating AAT protein and stopping liver accumulation.
    • BEAM-301 (In-Vivo GSDIa): Base editing to restore glucose-6-phosphatase activity in Glycogen Storage Disease Type Ia.
  • Corporate Alliances: Established collaboration with Eli Lilly (LLY), acquiring Verve Therapeutics' cardiovascular base editing programs.

5. Rocket Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (NASDAQ: RCKT)

  • Technology & Platform: The pure-play rare disease clinical consolidator utilizing both adeno-associated virus (AAV) for organ-specific gene therapy and lentiviral vectors (LVV) for ex-vivo hematopoietic stem cell disorders.
  • Pipeline Assets:
    • RP-A501 (Danon Disease): Recombinant AAV9 vector carrying the LAMP2B gene for Danon disease, a fatal, X-linked monogenic cardiomyopathy that causes heart failure and death in adolescent males. Currently in a registrational Phase 2 trial. Represents one of the highest-value clinical-stage AAV programs in industry.
    • Ex-Vivo LVV Rare Disease Franchise: RP-L201 (Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency-I / LAD-I), RP-L102 (Fanconi Anemia), and RP-L301 (Pyruvate Kinase Deficiency).
  • Vulnerabilities & Regulatory Headwinds: In June 2024, the FDA issued a Complete Response Letter (CRL) for RP-L201 (LAD-I) regarding chemistry, manufacturing, and controls (CMC) validation requirements, highlighting the heightened regulatory bar FDA CBER has instituted for commercial vector characterization.

6. Cabaletta Bio (NASDAQ: CABA) & Kyverna Therapeutics (NASDAQ: KYTX)

  • The Autoimmune CAR-T Vanguard: Cabaletta Bio and Kyverna represent the specialized clinical vanguard targeting the autoimmune cell therapy revolution.
  • Clinical Hypothesis: In systemic autoimmune diseases (SLE, Systemic Sclerosis, Myositis), autoreactive B cells and plasma cells drive autoantibody production and tissue damage. Monoclonal antibodies (e.g., rituximab) only deplete circulating B cells in peripheral blood, leaving deep-tissue lymphoid B cells intact. Autologous CD19 CAR-T cells penetrate deep lymphoid organs (spleen, lymph nodes, bone marrow), completely eradicating the autoreactive B-cell clones and allowing naive, non-autoreactive B cells to repopulate—effectively resetting the human immune system.
  • Lead Assets:
    • Cabaletta (CABA-201): 4-1BB CD19 CAR-T without a viral vector tag; actively dosing in lupus nephritis, systemic sclerosis, and dermatomyositis.
    • Kyverna (KYV-101): Fully human CD19 CAR-T engineered to minimize immunogenicity and reduce cytokine release syndrome (CRS).
  • Market Dynamics: If autoimmune CAR-T achieves multi-year drug-free remissions in Phase 2/3 trials, these small-caps command an addressable market orders of magnitude larger than relapsed oncology.

7. Iovance Biotherapeutics, Inc. (NASDAQ: IOVA)

1. In-Vivo Viral Gene Therapy (AAV)

  • Mechanism: Recombinant adeno-associated virus (rAAV) packages a functional therapeutic cDNA expression cassette driven by a promoter. The vector enters target tissue via receptor-mediated endocytosis, translocates to the nucleus, and uncoats.
  • Nuclear State: The transgene predominantly persists as an episomal concatemer outside the host chromosome, meaning it does not integrate into host DNA.
  • The Pediatric Dilution Paradox: Because episomes do not replicate during host cell division, in-vivo AAV in dividing pediatric tissues (e.g., infant liver) is gradually diluted out as the organ grows, causing loss of therapeutic efficacy over time.
  • The Anti-AAV Antibody Wall: Between 30% and 60% of the human population harbors neutralizing antibodies (NAbs) against wild-type AAV serotypes from natural environmental exposure. Patients with NAb titers $>1:5$ destroy the therapeutic vector before it transduces tissue, disqualifying them from treatment. Because the first therapeutic dose stimulates massive anti-capsid IgG responses, a patient can only ever be dosed once in their lifetime.

      2. B2M / CIITA Knockout: Eliminates HLA Class I and Class II expression to prevent the host's immune system from recognizing and destroying donor cells.
      3. CD47 Overexpression ("Don't Eat Me" signal): Protects HLA-deficient cells from Natural Killer (NK) cell-mediated lysis.

3. The Autoimmune Frontier: The Immunological Reset

In autoimmune disease, autoreactive B cells produce pathogenic autoantibodies (e.g., anti-dsDNA in lupus, anti-topoisomerase in systemic sclerosis). Monoclonal antibodies (rituximab, belimumab) leave reservoir B cells in the bone marrow and spleen intact.

  • Depth of Depletion: Autologous CD19 CAR-T cells penetrate dense secondary and tertiary lymphoid organs, executing total B-cell clearance.
  • Thymic Re-education: Once CAR-T cells clear, the patient's hematopoietic stem cells generate new, naive B-cell progenitors in an environment free of autoantigen-driven inflammatory signals. Long-term follow-up from pioneering academic cohorts (Mackensen et al., Erlangen) shows patients maintaining complete drug-free clinical remission for >3 years with protective vaccine antibodies preserved.

Chokepoint 1: Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Controls (CMC) as the Primary Regulatory Gate

In small molecules, analytical characterization is absolute. In cell and gene therapy, "the product is the process."

  • The FDA Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research (CBER) has issued more Complete Response Letters (CRLs) for CMC deficiencies than for clinical efficacy failures.
  • AAV Vector Purity: Bioreactor production generates empty capsids (capsids lacking the therapeutic DNA) and partially filled/degraded capsids. If empty-to-full ratios are too high, patients receive excess viral antigens without therapeutic benefit, triggering severe immune responses. Regulatory authorities mandate rigorous analytical assays (AUC, cryo-TEM, mass photometry) that small-cap biotechs struggle to scale.

Chokepoint 2: Vein-to-Vein Latency and Hospital Capacity

  • Even after FDA approval, a cell therapy cannot scale without a network of FACT-accredited Authorized Treatment Centers (ATCs).
  • Qualifying an ATC requires 6 to 12 months of legal, operational, and clinical training per hospital.
  • High ICU bed utilization and limited apheresis slots cap weekly patient throughput. If turnaround exceeds 25 days, patient dropouts spike.

Chokepoint 3: The $2M–$4M Reimbursement Gauntlet & The CMS CGT Access Model

  • The Medicaid Problem: Over 50% of US Sickle Cell Disease patients are covered by state Medicaid programs. Because state Medicaid budgets are legally bound to balance annually, treating 50 patients in a single state at $2.2M per patient ($110M aggregate) threatens to trigger state budgetary emergencies.
  • The Federal Solution (CMS CGT Access Model): Officially launched in January 2025 and operationalizing through 2026, the Center for Medicare and Medicaid Innovation (CMMI) established a multi-state pooled purchasing structure. Over 33 states, DC, and Puerto Rico (representing 84% of Medicaid SCD beneficiaries) have joined. Under this framework, CMS negotiates uniform, outcomes-based multi-year rebates directly with Vertex and bluebird bio: if a patient suffers vaso-occlusive crises (VOC) post-treatment, the manufacturer must refund a substantial portion of the drug's cost.

Chokepoint 4: Hospital Inpatient Cash Flow & Working Capital Strain

When a hospital administers an ultra-high-cost therapy during an inpatient admission, it bills under Medicare/Medicaid Severity Diagnosis Related Groups (MS-DRGs).

  • Although CMS provides New Technology Add-on Payments (NTAP) or outlier payments, hospitals must float the $2.2M–$3.2M purchase order on their commercial accounts payable for 60 to 120 days while waiting for insurance adjudication.
  • Many community and non-profit hospital balance sheets refuse to carry multi-million-dollar receivables risk for single patients, leading to administrative bottlenecks.

Chokepoint 5: Safety Overhangs — Hepatotoxicity & Secondary Malignancies

  • High-Dose AAV Hepatotoxicity: Systemic delivery exceeding $1 \times 10^{14}\text{ vector genomes/kg}$ floods the liver, activating cytotoxic T lymphocytes that destroy transduced hepatocytes, elevate liver transaminases (ALT/AST), and risk lethal acute liver failure (e.g., fatal toxicities observed in high-dose trials by Astellas, Pfizer, and Sarepta).
  • Secondary T-Cell Malignancies: In early 2024, the FDA mandated class-wide Boxed Warnings on all approved autologous CD19- and BCMA-directed CAR-T cell therapies regarding the risk of secondary T-cell malignancies arising from insertional oncogenesis of the viral vector. While the incidence rate remains tiny (<0.1%), the regulatory overhang enforces lifelong 15-year patient registries.
