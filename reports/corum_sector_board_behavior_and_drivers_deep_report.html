1.1 How the Corum Board Currently Classifies Sectors (MS-1 vs. MS-2.1 vs. decisions.py)

The Corum Sector Board tracks 192 sector and subsector rows (charted via liquid ETFs or equal-weighted stock baskets) and assigns every row two layers of designation:

  1. A 4-State Regime Call (decision): RIDE, FRAGILE, EARLY, or AVOID, governed by a $2 \times 2$ grid around a 0.55 threshold (CALL_LINE = 0.55, with a ±0.03 hysteresis band in banded_decision):
    • RIDE ($\text{Momentum} \ge 0.55,\ \text{Sustainability} \ge 0.55$): Trend is working and underlying structure is durable.
    • FRAGILE ($\text{Momentum} \ge 0.55,\ \text{Sustainability} < 0.55$): Price is moving up, but volatility is high, breadth is narrow, or structure is stretched.
    • EARLY ($\text{Momentum} < 0.55,\ \text{Sustainability} \ge 0.55$): Structure is intact/quiet, but price momentum has not crossed the line.
    • AVOID ($\text{Momentum} < 0.55,\ \text{Sustainability} < 0.55$): Both trend and structure are weak.
  2. A 5-State Action Bucket (bucket) in corum/foundry/decisions.py: BUY, WATCHING, HOLD, OVERHEATED, or REDUCE.

1.2 Five Empirical & Structural Flaws Discovered in the Live Board (2026-10-26)

By cross-examining the 10-year point-in-time backtest (docs/MOMENTUM_SUSTAINABILITY_V2.md), the 32-episode historical retro-test (docs/PREDICTIVE_FRAMEWORKS.md), the 12-session stability audit (docs/CALL_STABILITY_REVIEW.md), and the live 2026-10-26 snapshot in sector_command.json, we identify five fundamental flaws that prevent the current board from accurately designating how sectors behave:

Flaw 1: The Split-Brain Contradiction Between MS-2.1 (sector_command.py) and Action Buckets (decisions.py)

[!WARNING] 67% of live RIDE calls (42 out of 60 rows) are blocked from the BUY bucket.
In the 10-year empirical test (629 extended runs across 85 ETFs, 2016–2026), rows flagged extended (6m return or 200d extension at $\ge \text{p90}$ of their own history) beat the average row by +0.35% next month (~2 SE), and rolling_over extended runs had fewer worst-quintile drawdowns (9% vs. 24%, $-15\text{pp}$, Holm $p = 0.02$) and $+8.5\text{pp}$ excess return at $+126\text{d}$. Because price-only overheat flags were empirically anti-predictive of tops, MS-2.1 removed extended and rolling_over overrides from classify_v2.
However, decisions.py (bucket()) still executes if hot.get("rolling_over"): return "REDUCE" and if hot.get("extended"): return "OVERHEATED" BEFORE checking if d == "RIDE": return "BUY"!

Because sectors rotate on 3-to-9 month macro/inventory cycles rather than multi-year straight lines, omitting intermediate horizons produces two mirror-image errors:

    • Food distributors: 3m = -7.7%, yet dec = RIDE (mom = 0.626, sus = 0.571).
    • Pharma [XPH]: 1m = -5.2%, 3m = -7.6%, yet dec = RIDE (mom = 0.796, sus = 0.599).
    • Transports [IYT] (3m = -5.1%), Industrials (broad) [XLI] (3m = -5.1%), Banks (regional) [KRE] (3m = -4.4%), Malaysia [EWM] (1m = -3.7%, 3m = -4.3%, 6m = -7.2%), Travel/leisure [PEJ] (3m = -4.2%), P&C insurance [KBWP] (3m = -3.1%), Insurance [IAK] (3m = -2.9%), Steel [SLX] (1m = -2.7%, 3m = -2.3%, 6m = -0.5%). Flaw 4: The EARLY Paradox and the Empty WATCHING Bucket

In MS-2.1, EARLY ($\text{mom} < 0.55,\ \text{sus} \ge 0.55$) lost $-1.40%$ per quarter in out-of-sample testing—making it by far the worst-performing designation on the entire board, underperforming AVOID by $-1.31%$ per quarter!

  • Why MS-2.1 EARLY fails: When a sector has low momentum (mom < 0.55) but high sustainability_v2 (sus >= 0.55), it means the sector has low 1-year volatility and sits relatively close to its 52-week high, not that it is inflecting upward from a trough. On the live 2026-10-26 board, the 16 EARLY rows include REITs (broad) [XLRE] (3m = -6.6%), Residential REITs (3m = -8.2%), Industrial REITs [INDS] (3m = -7.3%), Restaurants (3m = -7.8%), and Saudi Arabia & Gulf [KSA] (3m = -4.6%, 6m = -9.7%, 12m = -12.5%). These are not early turnarounds; they are low-volatility slow bleeders rolling over from a plateau before their 252-day volatility or drawdown metrics have expanded enough to drop them into AVOID.
  • Why true early inflections are missed: A genuine historical sector inflection (e.g., Semiconductors in Nov 2022, Uranium in Aug 2021, Tankers in Feb 2022, Regional Banks in Nov 2023, Biotech in Nov 2023, Software in Q3 2026) begins after a high-volatility drawdown when 12m return is negative, 200d breadth is just crossing from 15% to 40%, and 252-day trailing volatility is still elevated. Under MS-2.1, every single true early inflection scores mom < 0.55 and sus < 0.55 (AVOID).
  • Why WATCHING has 0 rows on the live board: decisions.py assigns WATCHING only when a row is EARLY or AVOID, has macro_tailwind.score >= bucket_gate (75th percentile of positive tailwinds, 0.0175), mom < 0.55, AND sus >= 0.55. Because sus >= 0.55 is required, AVOID rows can never enter WATCHING (AVOID by definition has sus < 0.55 in MS-2.1!), and none of the 16 EARLY rows clear bucket_gate = 0.0175. Thus WATCHING is a dead code path (0 rows).

Flaw 5: Uniform Scoring Across Non-Uniform Sector Behavioral Archetypes

The deepest conceptual flaw is treating all 192 rows as if they obey the same return-generating stochastic process. Empirically (docs/PREDICTIVE_FRAMEWORKS.md), sectors behave according to five distinct structural archetypes, and the exact same price/volatility signature means opposite things across archetypes:

  • In a Secular Compounder (Cybersecurity, Waste, Drug Distributors), 12 consecutive up-months with low volatility and a new 52-week high (sus = 0.85) is a RIDE continuation signal.
  • In a Capex-Driven Commodity/Component Cyclical (Memory [MU], NAND flash, Shipping [BOAT], Lithium [LIT], Uranium [URA], Steel [SLX]), low trailing volatility and 12 consecutive up-months occur at peak cycle margins right as new supply/capex (Component I) is commissioned—1 to 3 quarters before a $-40%$ to $-70%$ cyclical bust! Conversely, its best EARLY / RIDE entry occurs when trailing 252-day volatility is high (sus < 0.35) and spot prices (Component F) first turn up while industry capex is still starved.

2.1 Cross-Sector Input-Output, Capex Propagation & Substitution Rings

Sectors are bound together in directed input-output chains where Sector A's capital expenditure is Sector B's revenue, and Sector C's output price is Sector D's margin compression:

  1. The AI & Electrification CapEx Cascade (7-Ring Propagation Lag):

    • Ring 0 (Cash-Flow Source): Megacap Platforms (MAGS: MSFT, META, GOOGL, AMZN) convert high-margin digital ad, search, and cloud software cash flows into $250B+/yr of AI infrastructure CapEx.
    • Ring 1 (Immediate Silicon & Packaging — 0 to 1 Quarter Lag): AI semiconductors [SMH] (NVDA, AVGO, AMD, TSM) and Memory [MU] (HBM3e/HBM4) capture immediate revenue hyper-growth, followed within 1–2 quarters by AI networking & optics (ANET, CRDO, COHR, LITE) and Hard-disk drives / NAND flash (WDC, STX, SNDK) for nearline data-lake storage.
    • Ring 2 (Wafer Fab & Metrology Tools — 1 to 3 Quarter Lag): As foundries (TSM) and memory makers (MU, SK Hynix) hit >90% utilization on CoWoS packaging and EUV litho, orders propagate to Semicap equipment (ASML, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, ONTO, CAMT).
    • Ring 3 (Physical Shells, Cooling & MEP Contracting — 2 to 4 Quarter Lag): Chips cannot run without energized shells and liquid-cooling loops, driving Datacenter REITs [DTCR] (EQIX, DLR), Specialty contractors (PWR, EME, FIX, MTZ), and HVAC & building climate (VRT, MOD, TT, CARR).
    • Ring 4 (Grid Equipment, Power Generation & Fuel — 3 to 8 Quarter Lag): 100MW–1GW training clusters hit regional transmission bottlenecks, driving multi-year backlogs in Grid & electrical equipment [GRID] (ETN, GEV, HUBB, PWR), behind-the-meter Merchant nuclear power (CEG, VST, TLN), Utilities [XLU], and Natural gas [FCG] / Midstream [AMLP] (pipeline lateral buildouts for CCGT gas turbines).
    • Ring 5 (Raw Physical Materials — 4 to 12 Quarter Lag): Grid expansion, transformers, and data centers require massive physical tonnage, tightening Copper miners [COPX] (FCX, SCCO) and Uranium mining [URNM].

2.3 Microeconomic & Industrial Organization Mechanics

Why do two subsectors facing the exact same macro shock behave completely differently? Because of six microeconomic structural parameters:

  1. CapEx Lead Time & Supply Elasticity (The Cobweb Theorem):
    • How long does it take to bring new capacity online when prices spike?
    • 0–6 Months: SaaS wrappers, digital ad inventory, staffing agencies.
    • 18–36 Months: Leading-edge semiconductor fabs (TSM), HBM cleanrooms (MU), LNG liquefaction trains, specialty electrical transformers (ETN, GEV), dry-bulk/container ships (BOAT).
    • 5–12 Years: Greenfield copper mines (COPX), uranium mines & SWU enrichment cascades (URNM, LEU), nuclear SMRs (NUKZ), solid rocket motor energetics (LHX, NOC), FDA-approved biologics (XBI).
    • Rule: The longer the CapEx/regulatory lead time, the longer and more violent the RIDE phase once utilization crosses 90%, and the deeper the subsequent bust if management over-orders capacity at the peak.
  2. Operating Leverage ($\Delta \text{EBIT} / \Delta \text{Revenue}$):
    • High fixed-cost, high depreciation/R&D industries (Memory, NAND, Airlines, Shipping, Steel, Hotels/Cruise Lines, Clinical Labs) experience explosive 300–800% EPS expansion on a 15–20% revenue increase—and equally violent EPS collapses when revenue drops 10%.
    • Asset-light tollbooths (Insurance brokers, Legal/Case-Law monopolies, Franchise restaurants, Credit ratings/Exchanges) have low operating variance and high floor margins.
  3. Pricing Power & Customer Switching Costs:
    • System-of-Record / Statutory Monopoly: Westlaw/Lexis citators (RELX, TRI), EDA chip design software (SNPS, CDNS), lithography (ASML), aerospace aftermarket PMA parts (TDG, HEI), municipal landfills (WM, RSG). They raise prices 4–8% annually regardless of GDP.
    • Commodity Price-Takers: Lithium miners, solar panel makers (TAN), commodity DRAM/NAND, dry-bulk shippers (BDRY), merchant E&Ps. They have zero pricing power; their margin is purely the spread between global spot price and their position on the first-to-fourth quartile cost curve (C1/AISC).
  4. Orderbook Visibility & Contract Structure:
    • Take-or-Pay / Fee-Based: Midstream [AMLP] (10–20 yr minimum volume commitments), Merchant nuclear PPAs (20 yr hyperscaler contracts), Datacenter REITs (7–15 yr leases with 3% escalators).
    • Cost-Plus vs. Fixed-Price (FPIF): In Defense [ITA], Cost-Plus R&D contracts protect margins during inflation, whereas Fixed-Price Incentive Fee (FPIF) development contracts (e.g., Boeing tanker/starliner, early-lot shipbuilding) generate billions in charge-offs when skilled labor and titanium costs inflate.
  5. Balance Sheet Duration & Refinancing Walls:
    • Sectors funded by short-term floating-rate debt or serial equity issuance (Mortgage REITs, Office REITs, Clean energy, pre-revenue Biotech / Longevity / Space / Quantum) face existential insolvency or dilutive death spirals when rates stay high.
  6. Supply of Paper (Net Share Issuance + SBC vs. Cannibal Buybacks):
    • Historically (PREDICTIVE_FRAMEWORKS.md), equity manias do not die from high valuation alone; they die when insider selling, secondary follow-ons, ATM issuance, IPOs/SPACs, and Stock-Based Compensation (SBC > 20% of revenue) overwhelm marginal buyer inflows. Conversely, sectors retiring 4–8% of their float annually (Free cash flow yield [COWZ], Energy [XLE], Big-box/Auto-parts retail, Drug distributors) create a mechanical EPS and price floor.
